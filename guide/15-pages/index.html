1. What this workspace is for

Understand the product before learning the menus.

The job it supports

Studio OS joins acquisition, client handoffs, service delivery, membership, retention and management review. Its central loop is notice → inspect evidence → choose an accountable next step → reassess.

The current state

This is a published shareholder-review prototype. The displayed people, staff, prices, capacity, dates, events and thresholds are fictional. Real private operational sources remain disconnected.

The portal boundary

This owner-facing workspace links outward to the separate Customer Portal. It does not add a route from that customer site into owner systems. A public page is not an access-controlled store for confidential records.

3. Today: notice and investigate

Use the queue to turn a dashboard into a next step.

Read the four cards

Current active members is a membership snapshot. Occupied places includes occupied demo bookings, with maintenance holds excluded from available capacity. Human-review signals counts clients with explainable signals. Awaiting reviews counts unacknowledged sample actions.

Read a queue row

Each action has a title, evidence, responsible role, due date and gate. Follow the title to its relevant screen. Treat the date as part of the fixed fictional snapshot.

Use the review control

Mark reviewed (demo) acknowledges the sample action. A reviewed label and browser log appear. No follow-up message, booking change, policy approval or equipment release results from this acknowledgement.

Cross-check the snapshot

Today’s sessions and latest evidence give context. The immutable seed event ledger is separate from browser simulation logs, so acknowledging an action does not create a completed visit or purchase.

4. Sales: acquisition and conversion

Compare sources without confusing enquiries with attended visits.

Read the source table

Sales & leads groups the fictional cohort by source: leads, completed first visits and second purchases. Lead → first uses leads as its denominator; first → second uses completed first visits.

Inspect a record

The conversion/service board carries goal or schedule preference, source, stage, next action and responsible role. Follow a client link to inspect lifecycle evidence.

Use appropriate conversations

The proposed handoff is consultative: understand a legitimate goal or preference, then review an owner-approved option. The demo does not collect contacts, edit customer details, send offers or take payment.

Avoid target invention

The source table shows a fictional example, not an approved acquisition target. Imported vendor conversion claims have not been silently applied as Paila performance thresholds.

5. Client journey and Customers

Follow one person’s lifecycle and service handoff.

Read the funnel correctly

Leads, completed first visits and second purchases are cumulative milestones. Current active membership is a state. Exited people can still belong to earlier conversion milestones. The card totals therefore should not be added as unique people.

Filter the board

Journey stage and Lead source change which rows appear. Reset them to All stages / All sources to see the whole cohort. Inspect client selects an individual independently of these board filters.

Read the detail

The detail card shows a fictional goal, responsible role, first/latest completed visit, remaining credits, next action, review signals and dated events. It deliberately avoids contact and health narratives.

Use Customers consistently

Customers renders the same people and projection as the journey board. It is another review view, not a second independent customer database or live CRM.

6. Classes: session selection and check-in

Learn the difference between arrival and completed attendance.

Select a time

Press a session button. The view shows fictional capacity, capacity after holds, occupied places, open spaces, waitlist count, instructor example and readiness context.

Use scheduled-session controls

A currently Booked alias in a Scheduled session offers Check in, Mark no-show and Cancel demo booking. Each transition changes only the fictional browser session.

Know what check-in means

Check-in is arrival. It does not create a completed visit event or deduct credits. Reports remain based on the immutable completed-visit seed, so a check-in need not change reported visits.

Read completed sessions

The completed 09:00 example is read-only. There is no button to rewrite its historical attendance. Instructor labels are fictional readiness examples, not actual credential checks.

7. Waitlists, holds and capacity

Try the available-place rule without bypassing a safety gate.

Read effective capacity

Available capacity is fictional capacity minus held places. Occupied places include Booked, Checked in and Attended aliases. Cancelled or No-show aliases do not occupy a place in this demo.

Promote only with room

The 17:30 example starts full with a waiting alias. Cancel a fictional booking, then use Promote if space on the waiting alias. Occupancy should return to the available limit, not exceed it.

Keep inspection holds

The 16:00 example has one held place. This reduces availability even when a client wants that slot. The tutorial and booking controls cannot approve equipment release.

Separate bookings from charges

A demo no-show or cancellation carries no fee, refund or real booking-provider effect. Terms and safety decisions need their own approved authority.

Try this

After resetting, demonstrate why the full session cannot promote a waiting alias until an occupied place is freed.

8. Memberships and passes

Review entitlements, options and exceptions respectfully.

Read the review table

Memberships & passes displays a fictional person, membership or intro state, option reference, remaining credits and exception/next review. A purchase followed by a later exit is not an active member.

Interpret credits

The seed supplies fictional purchase credits and completed visits. Remaining credits are a demonstration projection, not an actual prepaid balance from a booking or accounting provider.

Interpret price cards

The first-visit, four-visit and eight-visit options use illustrative NPR amounts. They are not approved prices, validity periods, cancellation terms or contractual entitlements.

Review requests separately

Client 10’s freeze request is evidence awaiting human terms review. Client 08’s cancellation retains a neutral schedule-related reason. No button executes an actual membership freeze, refund or renewal.

9. Retention and service recovery

Use visible reasons instead of a mysterious risk score.

Read the signal

The queue explains participation gaps, unused credits and booking friction. Follow the client link to inspect the dated context. A record appearing here is not proof it will churn.

Adjust a scenario

Use a whole number from 7 through 45 in Prototype lapse window and press Apply demo window. This affects the gap signal. Unused-credit or friction reasons can remain even if the gap rule no longer triggers.

Review human context

Check the responsible role and next action. Permission, member autonomy, legitimate preferences and an approved suitable option matter before any real outreach. No message or contact collection is performed here.

Respect neutral exits

Freeze/cancellation review keeps a service or schedule reason visible without health narratives. The proposed before/during/after service checklist is a review idea; procedure approval still lives in the SOP process.

11. Reports and denominators

Read counts and time windows before interpreting percentages.

Choose the window

Daily means the fictional snapshot date; Weekly means seven days ending at that date; Last 30 days means thirty days ending there. Read the exact start/end dates on screen.

Read cohort conversion

The conversion denominator uses leads acquired in that window and their outcomes observed by the snapshot. This is different from counting every first visit or purchase that happened in the window.

Read membership movement

Current active members is a snapshot measure. Starting-member churn uses members active before the window and cancellations from that starting group. If there is no denominator, the display is n/a.

Read the event ledger

The ledger exposes fictional dated evidence for visits, purchases and cancellations. Recorded cash receipts add purchase amounts. They do not establish earned revenue, deferred credits, tax or profit.

Keep arrival separate

Check-in changes in Classes do not rewrite the completed-visit seed. Likewise, marking a management review complete does not create a purchase in Reports.

12. Management and financial scenarios

Turn one constraint into a reviewable experiment.

Read the constraint card

The proposed preferred-time capacity constraint cites session occupancy, waitlist and booking-friction evidence. It has a hypothesis, experiment, responsible role, reassessment date and gate.

Review before implementation

Check coverage, approved capacity, cost and owner authority before a real schedule, price or staffing change. Mark reviewed (demo) on the action board only acknowledges the simulation.

Change one numeric input

The financial card offers fixed monthly cost, average monthly price, variable cost percentage and active members. It calculates contribution, break-even members, model revenue/cost and surplus or shortfall.

Understand the calculation

Contribution equals price multiplied by one minus the variable-cost fraction. Break-even membership rounds fixed cost divided by contribution upward. Model surplus is contribution times members minus fixed cost.

Respect the limits

Inputs are fictional assumptions, not Paila budgets or approved targets. The scenario excludes tax, capital spending, debt, deferred revenue and refunds. It does not reconcile an actual accounting system or classify Paila into an external revenue stage.

Use optional planning views without implying business approval.

Choose the lens

Read dependency gates

The workstreams cover business/funding, premises/procurement, staffing/service, presale/first visit and policies/integrations. Owner or source approval is still required for the corresponding real decisions.

Use the review cadence

Read automation candidates

Automations lists trigger/dependency, human review gate and inactive state. No production messages, charges, staffing or safety actions execute. Prototype button interactions are not an activated private automation system.

14. SOPs, sources and research use

See what informed the design and what still needs approval.

Find a procedure

The SOP master register supports search and status filters. Existing procedure links open the preserved handbook. Draft / Planned statuses and missing approval metadata are not silently promoted to Approved.

Inspect research application

Systems & data sources shows a research-to-feature map and evidence-quality table. The videos and parsed documents inform lifecycle, sales, retention, daily readiness, constraints and launch-planning patterns.

Read source quality

VID-005 uses the official workshop-page summary only; a full workshop transcript was not available in that evidence record. Vendor and external numerical claims are not local studio targets.

Distinguish sources from systems

The historical Benchmark model is reference context. The research map explains design decisions. The data dictionary describes future private contracts; the fictional seed uses a separate demonstration schema. None connects the private backend by itself.

Avoid “all research is policy”

Research-informed UI, a proposed SOP and an approved operating policy are different states. Some decisions deliberately remain human-gated or unapproved, even when their supporting idea is well documented.

15. Reset, troubleshoot and review with the owner

Finish with a safe, repeatable demonstration.

Know what persists

Review acknowledgements, demo booking statuses, unblocked operational checks, lapse-window setting, planning lens and the fictional action log persist in this browser tab’s session. They are not shared with other staff or customers.

Know what is page-local

Journey/source filters, selected client/session, report tabs and financial-scenario inputs are page-local view choices. Reloading returns those view choices to their defaults; it does not necessarily clear stored demo actions.

Reset deliberately

Press Reset fictional demo on a workspace screen to restore stored demo state. If browser storage is unavailable, demo changes may last only on the current page. No private data or real provider record is changed.

If something looks missing

Return stage/source filters to All, inspect the displayed sample date and review the relevant event ledger. A completed session is read-only; a full or held session cannot promote a waitlisted alias. n/a is a missing denominator, not a broken percentage.

If the workspace fails to load

The guides remain readable. Check JavaScript is enabled, reload the public URL and retry the workspace. The fallback offers the handbook and layout. Report the screen, visible message and attempted action; include no private client details.

Finish an owner walkthrough

Show one enquiry-to-first-visit handoff, one capacity guard, one explainable retention signal and one management experiment. Ask the owner to review usability and approve real terms separately. Publishing this guide does not authorise a formal new release.

Demonstrate the loop, reset the demo and name what still needs human approval.
